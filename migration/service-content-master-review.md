Research date: 2026-10-18 | 83 page outlines | 39 service-intent families | 166 competitor comparisons | 152 distinct reviewed pages

## How to use this report

Review the page’s role first, then its three prioritized gaps and proposed section outline. Approve the direction, remove services you do not offer, and identify the evidence needed. Shared family recommendations are adapted by page role and city focus; they are not instructions to publish city-name substitutions.

## Research method and limits

- Research date: October 18, 2026. Coverage: all 83 remaining URLs in the current service editorial inventory, including city hubs, cost pages, calculators and specialty service pages. /roofing/ is excluded as the approved reference. Product catalogs, individual products, company pages and general articles are outside this service-page report.
- Baseline: current Astro editorial data and all 83 rendered draft pages, each returning HTTP 200. Public links identify the preserved routes; the coverage findings describe the Astro draft captured for this report.
- Each URL received a service-and-location search. The report selects two relevant businesses from the returned organic results, reviews their accessible page content, and records the exact search phrases. Follow-up searches narrowed irrelevant restoration results, filtered directories or replaced inaccessible pages. The source ledger and saved captures preserve those decisions.
- These are prominent search-visible comparison pages, not a certified Google rank report. Results vary by engine, location, personalization and date; ads/maps and a fixed top-three position were not verified. Returned positions are recorded in the working ledger only, not presented as Google rankings.
- Two reviewed competitor links are supplied for every page. Some are regional, multi-service or contractor-selection benchmarks because that is what the query surfaced. Limited depth and redirected regional destinations are identified where relevant. Search-result snippets alone were not accepted as the final comparison evidence.
- Competitor descriptions are observations of what their pages discuss. Their prices, promises, credentials, response times and technical claims were not independently established and are not proposed Roof King claims. Outlines are original editorial recommendations, pending business and technical verification.
- Gap labels describe information missing or insufficiently developed in the current service copy. Shared reviews, galleries and forms do not establish service-specific depth or local job provenance. The findings are not a traffic, backlink, conversion or Search Console audit.

## Main findings

### Explain the work, not just the next conversation

Many current pages tell visitors to ask about materials, preparation or installation. Replace that pattern with a clear explanation, then use a real example to support it. More words alone will not solve the depth problem.

### Commercial pages need commercial subject matter

Roof-types must actually explain the confirmed systems. Commercial storm and siding pages need building assemblies, business operations, assessment and owner-facing records instead of residential copy with a few nouns changed.

### Cost and calculator pages need useful interpretation

Provide measured examples, inclusions and assumptions. Publish numbers only after the owner approves recent examples and a review date. Explain the actual Roofr output and keep its preliminary status clear.

### City pages need first-hand local evidence

The 24 city hubs currently reuse the same four short topic descriptions. Add real jobs, material choices, attributable reviews and confirmed availability. City history, unsupported weather claims and neighborhood lists cannot substitute for experience.

### Keep service decisions separate from product specifications

Service pages explain assessment, installation, care and the decision to hire. Use short material previews and link to the established Products hierarchy for brands, colors, ratings, accessories and exact specifications.

### Protect the role of every existing URL

Keep every URL. Distinguish the Columbus company landing page, local services, Dayton hiring/service pages, Dublin specialties and two calculators through their content roles and internal links. No deletions, redirects or canonical changes are proposed.

## Recommended writing order

1. Confirm technical/business facts and fill the main service hubs: commercial roofing, siding, gutters, decks and storm damage.
2. Build specialist depth: commercial systems/repair/replacement/storm, residential repairs/replacement/metal, siding installation, decks, insulation and windows.
3. Approve the cost examples and estimator explanations.
4. Add verified city projects and finish each local page with its distinct role.

The priority labels below describe content need, not forecast search traffic. No arbitrary word-count target is proposed. A page is ready when the user’s questions are answered and the work is supported by accurate examples.

## Decisions and evidence needed

- Confirm actual services, systems, brands and accessories before adding them as offers. Commercial membranes/coatings, insulation methods, window products, deck accessories and emergency equipment need explicit confirmation.
- Approve recent measured estimate examples for the cost pages, or choose a useful non-numeric cost-factor format. Do not use competitors’ published rates as Roof King prices.
- Supply approved project stories with location, dates, products, preparation and photo permissions. A shared gallery image needs confirmed job details before being presented as local evidence.
- Verify response hours, scheduling language, workmanship terms, manufacturer eligibility and registration steps. Avoid importing competitors’ guarantees or deadlines.
- For technical, energy, approval or insurance-policy detail, verify current manufacturer/official sources during writing. This report recommends topics; it does not establish those requirements.

## Component and linking direction

Parent service pages can introduce actual children with the approved service selector. Leaf pages should use visible topic-specific rows, media and explanations, with no child-selector component. Reuse layout and data structures while writing distinct content. Avoid repeating a generic closing three-link section.

Link detailed product selection to the Products pillar. Use local pages to explain relevant property/project decisions and documented local work. Do not reproduce a complete product catalog in every city.
