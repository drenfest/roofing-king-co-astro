What to Do First When a Storm Damages Your Roof
Stay off the roof and avoid hazards
Wet shingles and storm debris make roof access dangerous. If a tree has struck the house, a ceiling is sagging or
water is near wiring, keep people away from the affected area. Contact emergency services or the utility company
when conditions require it. Do not climb onto the roof to take photographs.
Photograph the damage from a safe location
Take pictures of the house from the ground, including the roof edges, gutters, siding and fallen debris. Photograph
interior stains or active leaks as well. Record the storm date and save the original photos. These details help
explain what changed and where an inspector should look.
Protect the interior without creating another hazard
Move belongings away from active leaks when it is safe to do so. If temporary protection is needed, ask a qualified
contractor what can be done safely. Keep receipts and photographs of any temporary work. Temporary protection
is not a permanent roof repair.
Arrange an inspection before deciding on replacement
One missing shingle does not automatically mean the entire roof needs to be replaced. The right recommendation
depends on the extent of the damage, the roof's age and condition, and whether water reached the materials
underneath.




Signs of Wind Damage on a Residential Roof
Wind commonly affects roof edges, ridges and areas around chimneys or other projections. Some damage is
visible from the yard; other conditions require a trained inspection.
 Missing, displaced or creased shingles.
 Lifted shingles or broken sealant bonds that may be difficult to see from the ground.
 Loose ridge caps, drip edge or flashing around chimneys, skylights and vents.
 Shingle pieces, nails or roofing debris found in the yard.
 Gutters or siding pulled loose by wind or falling limbs.
A shingle that has lifted and settled back into place may still have a compromised seal. That is one reason a roof
can leak during the next storm even when no shingles appear to be missing.
What Hail Damage Looks Like
Hail can affect shingles, roof vents, soft-metal flashing and gutters. Dents in metal are worth documenting, but they
do not by themselves prove that every nearby shingle is damaged. Hail size, roof age, material and the angle of
impact all matter.
On asphalt shingles, an inspector looks for impact-related surface changes and distinguishes them from normal
wear, blistering, foot traffic or older damage. A proper assessment explains the location and significance of the
findings rather than relying on a single close-up photo.
If you see dented gutters, granules near downspouts or marks on roof accessories after a hailstorm, report them
when requesting the inspection. Avoid rubbing, lifting or testing shingles yourself.
Roof Leaks After Heavy Rain or Fallen Debris
Water can enter at a damaged shingle, flashing connection, roof valley, pipe boot or chimney intersection. The
ceiling stain may be several feet from the actual opening because water can travel along framing before becoming
visible.
Fallen limbs may puncture roofing or damage the decking underneath. If a tree is resting on the house, leave
removal and structural evaluation to qualified professionals. The roof repair estimate should account for any damage
discovered after the obstruction is safely removed.
A roof leak also can reveal an older defect rather than new storm damage. We distinguish observed storm-related
conditions from pre-existing wear as carefully as the available evidence allows.

Storm Damage Roof Repair or Replacement?
When a repair may be enough
If damage is limited to a few shingles or a flashing detail and the surrounding roof remains serviceable, a repair may
be the sensible choice. The replacement shingles and repair method still need to be compatible with the existing
roof.
When replacement deserves consideration
Replacement may be appropriate when damage is spread across multiple roof slopes, the existing shingles are
brittle or deteriorated, or water has affected a larger portion of the roof assembly. The decision should be based on
documented conditions, not simply the fact that a storm occurred.

Residential Storm Damage FAQs
Can wind damage a roof without blowing shingles off?
Yes. Wind can lift shingles and weaken their sealant bonds without leaving a visible gap from the ground. An
inspection can help determine whether the affected shingles remain serviceable.
Do hail dents on gutters mean my roof needs replacement?
No. Dents are evidence that hail struck a surface, but the roof covering needs its own assessment. Metal marks
alone do not establish that every shingle has functional damage.
How soon should I schedule a roof inspection after a storm?
Arrange an inspection reasonably promptly if you see missing shingles, loose flashing, fallen debris or interior water
staining. If there is an immediate safety hazard, address that first.
Will homeowners insurance pay for storm damage roof repairs?
It may, depending on your policy and the cause and extent of the damage. The insurance company determines
coverage. Roof King can document roofing conditions and provide a repair or replacement estimate.
Can Roof King repair storm-damaged siding and gutters too?
Roof King offers roofing, siding and gutter services. The assessment can identify related exterior damage so the
appropriate work is described and priced separately.
Does a small roof leak mean I need a new roof?
Not necessarily. A localized flashing or shingle problem may be repairable. Repeated leaks, widespread storm
damage or deteriorated roofing materials can change the recommendation.
Is a free inspection the same as an insurance adjustment?
No. A roofing inspection addresses the condition of the roof and possible construction work. Your insurer evaluates
coverage under the policy.
